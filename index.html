Chapter One — 1947

A Legend Is
Born in Fire

When the family arrived in Lahore in 1947, they carried one thing that mattered: a single iron karahi and a recipe that refused to be written down. Nearly eight decades later, the same fire still burns at Lakshmi Chowk.

Every karahi is finished to order — fresh ginger, green chillies, desi ghee, and meat that never sees a freezer. No shortcuts. No frozen stock. Just the patience of three generations in every sizzle.

The Butt Family

Chapter Two — The Method

Iron, Ghee
& Fire

A real karahi is theatre. The iron pan arrives roaring, gravy thickened only by tomatoes and time — never a spoonful of gravy powder. The aroma hits you before the dish touches the table.

Our mutton comes from trusted suppliers every single morning. The masala is ground fresh daily. And the naan? Pulled from the tandoor seconds before your karahi lands.

Chapter Three — The Grill

Charcoal &
Smoke

The tandoor beside the karahi station never sleeps. Seekh kababs rolled by hand, malai boti marinated overnight, chops kissed by charcoal — the BBQ menu is the quiet second legend of Lakshmi Chowk.

Regulars order both: a karahi for the table, and kababs for the wait.
